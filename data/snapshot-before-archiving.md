## Average watch time is the diagnostic, not 3-second hold

On a roughly 70-second clip, the Oz post holds a viewer 28.5 seconds and the
other three lose them inside 9. That gap is far louder than the hold figures
and it is the number that separates a clip someone watched from a clip nobody
watched. 3-second hold measures the thumbnail. Average watch time measures
whether there is anything in the video.

## This retires the stray-captions theory

I had flagged the unexplained "_OpusClip Captions" suffix as the likely cause
of the two worst holds on record. The Oz clip carries the same suffix and holds
51.4% at 28.5 seconds, which is the account's second best watch time. The
suffix is not the problem. The footage is.

## So the rights policy is not a performance argument, and I should not sell it as one

I recommended moving back to interview and documentary clips and cited Deakins,
Cronenweth and Booth as evidence that it is the account's best form. Within this
week that claim does not hold: the best post is studio footage and the weakest
two are also studio footage, which says the form is high variance rather than
bad. Mary Poppins outperforms every interview clip on record except Deakins.

The case for the policy rests on two things that have nothing to do with reach.
A worldwide block is an account-level risk rather than a lost post, and an
interview clip is the only kind I can verify from here, because its transcript
is readable and its footage is not. Both reasons stand. The performance reason
does not, and pretending otherwise would make the next bad week unreadable.

What to watch, now that the policy has changed: whether interview-form posts
clear roughly 2,000 views, which is where the studio-footage posts other than
Mary Poppins sat, and whether any of them reach her share count. If the first
four interview posts come in under that, the policy is costing reach and the
honest move is to say so rather than defend it.
