Jack Cardiff, BSC shot Black Narcissus (1947) for Michael Powell and Emeric Pressburger in three-strip Technicolor, taking his lighting model from seventeenth century painting rather than studio colour practice. Vermeer is the painter Cardiff names for this film specifically, because the light had to be clear and as simple as possible, with Rembrandt supplying the alternation of light against dark that gives a figure depth.

The choice ran against the process. Three-strip Technicolor was notorious for the volume of light it demanded, and Cardiff worked the convent interiors at lower levels than the stock was generally held to tolerate. None of it was shot in the Himalayas. The palace at Mopu was built at Pinewood, and the mountain distances behind it are matte paintings and painted backdrops, several angled to catch real daylight.

Cardiff is clear about where the analogy fails. A painter composes a fixed surface, while actors move through a lit space and walk into the instruments, and a cinematographer routinely begins on a close-up and tracks back out of his own lighting. The Vermeer reference describes a principle of simplicity rather than a transferable setup, which is why it survives the move from a still image to a moving one.
