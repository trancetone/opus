Michael Chapman photographed Raging Bull (1980) from Life magazine, specifically the big flash-lit prize-fight stills of the nineteen forties and the work of Weegee, on the reasoning that this is how his generation and Martin Scorsese's actually remember fights. Inside the ring he and Scorsese set a rule that the fighting itself runs at twenty-four frames a second, real time, and that the overcranked footage is reserved for the moments when Jake LaMotta is not fighting, in the corner or breathing against the ropes. The changes happen inside single shots rather than between them, so LaMotta knocks a man down at twenty-four frames, crosses to the neutral corner at forty-eight, and returns to twenty-four when the fight resumes, with the camera panning a full circle one way as he moves the other. Tying frame rate to whether a man is currently throwing punches makes the film's sense of time a property of the boxing rather than a stylistic layer over it, which is why the slow footage never reads as slow motion.
