Owen Roizman photographed The Taking of Pelham One Two Three (1974), and the problem he describes is that the subway car had to switch from its normal lighting to emergency lighting partway through, which meant going from built-in fluorescents that took care of themselves to a level far too dim to hold a face. His gaffer pulled bulbs out of the existing fixtures and put small twenty and forty watt incandescents in their place, and then, because that left nothing at all on the actors, they taped dentist mirrors to the backs of the poles inside the car and set twenty-five watt mushroom bulbs against them. The mirrors are the part worth stealing, since a mirror the size of a coin can be hidden on something already in the frame and will throw a usable amount of light at a face from a position no lamp could occupy. Those bulbs read far too red at normal supply, so he had the voltage pushed up to a hundred and forty or a hundred and eighty depending on the scene, which raises both the brightness and the colour temperature out of the same adjustment. The producer Edgar Scherick sent him a note during the shoot saying that with all due respect to the Prince of Darkness, he was paying Robert Shaw seventy-five thousand dollars and would like just once to see his eyes, and Roizman's account of receiving it is that he knew then that he was doing it right.
