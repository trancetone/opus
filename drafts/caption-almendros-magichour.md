Nestor Almendros shot Days of Heaven (1978) for Terrence Malick almost entirely in the twenty to twenty five minutes after the sun goes down, and he is blunt that the industry name for it oversells the window: magic hour is a euphemism, because it is not an hour.

What is left once the sun is gone is a sky that still carries light with no source in it, which reads as very soft and directionless on a face. Almendros wanted that because the film is a period picture and period interiors had no electricity, so his rule was that light should come from the windows, the way people actually lived. The production moved to Alberta rather than the Texas panhandle the story is set in, because at that latitude the window runs roughly twice as long.

It bought about twenty useful minutes a day. Almendros won the Academy Award for it and did not shoot the last three weeks.
