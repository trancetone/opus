Anne V. Coates, who cut Lawrence of Arabia for David Lean, describes the film's most quoted transition as something the equipment let them see by accident rather than a choice either of them made in advance. Her account is that she would normally have done the dissolve in the machine, which means the straight cut between the two shots would never have been screened at all, and she says she wonders whether they would have found it had they been working digitally. Because the sequence was cut on film and held together with the old Chinagraph marks, they took it into the theatre and ran the whole thing as assembled, and she and Lean looked at each other at the same moment and said it was a wonderful cut. She adds that she had always been slightly worried about how long the shots were held, and that when they tried shortening the sequence during the later reconstruction it did not work and went back the way it was.
