Jack Cardiff, who photographed Black Narcissus for Michael Powell, describes shooting what he considered the best photographic scene in the picture, the explanatory sequence where rain runs down the windows and he threw the shadows of that rain across every face in the room. A week or two later, on a location near the studio, the shot was the nuns leaving for Calcutta with two fire engines standing just out of frame on the right, hoses aimed upward so the water would fall into the lake as torrential rain. His own addition was to stand on a six foot ladder with a cup of water and tip it slowly in front of the lens, so that the camera could pan up through the drips to the mass of water coming down. Powell saw the rushes, said he would use that shot and cut the other scene completely, which is how Cardiff's best work in the film was removed by Cardiff's own idea.
