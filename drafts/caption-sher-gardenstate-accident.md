Lawrence Sher photographed Garden State (2004) and keeps a shot in it that nobody designed, which arrived while the crew was repositioning a lighting unit toward the wall of windows on the right of the set with the lamp still plugged in and burning as it travelled. The automatic doors of the building happened to open as the light went past them, so the beam went through the open doorway and across the space in a way that no lighting plan had called for, and his account of what he did next is simply that he said stop and told them not to move that light. What lifts this above an anecdote is that the decision cost something real, because a crew in the middle of a move has somewhere to be and the instruction to freeze a lamp in a doorway spends the rest of the day differently on the strength of something nobody asked for. The accident itself is free and happens on every set, which is the reason the story is about recognition rather than about luck, and recognition is the part that can actually be practised. Sher went on to build ShotDeck, a searchable library of film frames that grew out of the lookbooks he was assembling for this same picture, which suggests the same habit running underneath both: look hard at the frame in front of you, and keep the ones that work.
