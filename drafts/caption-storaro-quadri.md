Vittorio Storaro shot the killing of Professor Quadri for Bernardo Bertolucci's The Conformist (1970) at the Abetone, in the mountains of northern Italy, in winter, on the last afternoon the production had there before it was due back in Rome.

Bertolucci judged the sequence complex and the necessary coverage already in hand. Storaro, watching it, arrived at a crane movement that follows the husband as he leaves the car and holds him while the assassins close in from the trees with daggers and stab him repeatedly in the open. Storaro has said the staging reminded him of the killing of Julius Caesar, and that he felt in the moment it was the right thing to do.

The scene is built on the 1937 murders of Carlo and Nello Rosselli, anti-fascist exiles killed by French militants. Moving the camera rather than cutting keeps the victim and his killers inside one continuous frame, which is what makes the killing read as a public execution rather than an ambush.
