Shelley Duvall and Jack Nicholson were shot repeatedly on an 18mm lens through The Shining (1980), and Duvall's account is that they both dreaded hearing Stanley Kubrick call for it. She quotes the instruction as he gave it on set: give me the 18 in the tube.

An 18mm is very wide, and at the distance a close-up requires it bows a face outward from the centre and enlarges whatever sits nearest the glass. Duvall's summary is that the lens makes furniture look great and does faces no favours. She and Nicholson asked repeatedly for a 50 or a 75. John Alcott, who was lighting the picture, put in a word for them more than once. Kubrick did not relent.

Her reading is that the wrongness was the point rather than a compromise. A focal length short enough to bend a face makes a performance uncomfortable to watch without the actor doing anything differently.
