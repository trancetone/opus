Newton Thomas Sigel photographed the revival of the Superman franchise in 2005 and says he was intimidated by it, which is the useful starting point, because the format question he then had to settle turned out not to be about which camera made the best image. The top of the pyramid at the time was sixty-five millimetre film, and he ruled it out for a reason that has nothing to do with the negative, namely that there were no theatres that were going to show the result at seventy. A format is only as good as the last link in the chain that delivers it, so a gain recorded on the negative and then thrown away by the projector is not a gain at all but a cost the audience never sees returned. What he did instead was test a prototype digital camera that Panavision was developing, and the reason he gives for choosing it is neither convenience nor resolution but that the digital tools would let him push the picture towards the feeling of a comic book, somewhere in the direction of Dick Tracy. He then went back to shooting film afterwards, which is worth noticing, because it marks the decision as specific to one picture rather than a conversion, and a cinematographer who can pick the lesser image on purpose when it buys a particular look is describing control rather than preference.
