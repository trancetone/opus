An editor's account of Under Siege 2 (1995) is that Steven Seagal was admitted to the cutting room to work on the action sequences, and that on arriving he asked for reel one, meaning he intended to go through the entire picture rather than the fights. The editor describes finding himself with his arm pinned behind his head during one of those sessions while Seagal demonstrated a move on him, and notes drily that Seagal is a large man who also carries a gun. The structural problem he identifies is not the intimidation but the authority, because an actor in the cutting room sets up an antagonistic relationship with the director and leaves the editor adjudicating between the two of them. He also describes a comedian who held final cut over his own scenes and used it to remove every pratfall he had performed, having decided he would rather be a Cary Grant style comedian, which is what happens when the person with the most invested in a performance is handed control of it.
