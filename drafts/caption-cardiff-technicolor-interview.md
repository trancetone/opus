Jack Cardiff was a camera operator at Denham when Technicolor came to the studio to interview operators and choose one to train in the process, and the men who went in ahead of him came out shaken by how hard the technical questions were. Cardiff opened by saying that on the technical side he was absolutely a zero, and when the panel asked how he expected to get on in the film business at all, he answered that he studied painting, and light, and the behaviour of light inside buildings. They asked him which side of the face Rembrandt lit, he guessed, noting that an etching would reverse it, and then talked about Pieter de Hooch's interiors and the camera obscura until the interview ran out. He was chosen the next day, which is the clearest statement anyone has made about what Technicolor actually needed when it set up in Britain, not a man who could explain the three-strip camera but one who could decide where the light should come from.
