Vittorio Storaro lit the Rome half of The Conformist (1970) to an instruction he gave Bernardo Bertolucci out loud: let's create a kind of cage around this character, and use the light in so sharp a way that there is no embrace and no harmony between light and shadows.

Bertolucci took it. The film was already committed to showing an idea through symbols rather than stating it, which is the condition under which light can carry a character's politics rather than describe a room. Marcello Clerici has handed his judgement to the fascist state, and the hard separation of light from shadow holds him inside that decision in every Roman interior.

Storaro built the scheme to be withdrawn. Once Clerici travels to France the light begins to embrace the shadow, the separation softens, and colour enters. Applying a rule across one half of a film and dropping it in the next is what makes it read as a decision rather than a look.
