Dede Allen, who cut Bonnie and Clyde (1967) and Dog Day Afternoon (1975), used to tell the editors she worked with that she cut with her gut, a phrase repeated in this sequence by one of them as a description of his own method. The demonstration that follows shows what the gut is actually adjudicating, which is a mismatch: before the cut one actor is looking toward the lens, and after it his eyeline has moved up and away to the left. Rather than lose the take, the fix is to bring a second actor across the foreground so that his head passes over the first actor's at the moment of the join, which hides the discrepancy while the audience is looking at something else. Three frames are then removed to settle the rhythm, which is the scale most editing decisions are made at, well below the level at which a viewer could name what changed.
