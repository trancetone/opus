Conrad Hall was lighting the scene in In Cold Blood (1967) where Robert Blake, as Perry Smith, waits to be hanged and talks to the chaplain, a sequence shot on a stage with a rain gutter rigged above the window and a fan set to one side. The fan was not blowing the rain at the glass but the spray coming off it, and while Hall was watching, the light passing through that moving water happened once to fall across Blake's face in a way nobody had planned. He told Richard Brooks to watch the actor's face on the next rehearsal, and what the camera recorded was water running down Blake's cheeks while he spoke about his father and played the scene flat and unemotional. Hall's own account of the most asked-about shot he ever made is that he did not conceive it, Brooks did not conceive it, nobody conceived it, and that the visuals were crying for a character who would not cry himself.
