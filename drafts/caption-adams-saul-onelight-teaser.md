The shadow line across the wall behind the actors in this Better Call Saul scene has two edges, and Marshall Adams, who photographed the show, points out that the lower one is noticeably sharper than the one above it. The softer edge is thrown by the building's real roofline, which was already cutting the single source he was working with but did not sit high enough to land where he wanted it, so instead of adding a second unit he stood a sixteen-foot length of Duvetyne on stands just outside the frame and used it as a teaser to crop the same beam again, much closer in. A flag placed near the light softens the edge it makes and a flag placed near the subject sharpens it, which is why a piece of cloth a few feet off camera reads harder than a roofline sitting much further back. Everything in the shot is coming off that one unit raised on a lift, with a double net pulling the foreground back so the nearer figures do not go hot, and Adams says he is a firm believer that you can get away with much less light as long as there is a twinkle in somebody's eye. What is transferable is that the number of lights and the number of shadow edges are not the same count, because one source cut twice at two different distances will produce edges that look like they came from different places.
