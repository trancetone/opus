Ben Burtt, who invented every spoken dialect in the Star Wars films, is shown here teaching Ewokese to John Williams, and the method he describes amounts to an argument against invention. His starting point is a technical constraint, which is that raising the pitch of an ordinary speaking voice, his own or Jim Bloom's, pushes it toward Chip and Dale rather than toward anything the ear accepts as a language with speakers. The conclusion he draws is that it is easier to record a real language and have a performer deliver it than to build one from nothing, because a made-up language tends to come out as English rearranged, which listeners identify almost immediately as Pig Latin. What he does instead is take interesting words out of existing languages and mix them, Swahili against Chinese, with no attempt at literal meaning, and he rules out reversed speech on the same grounds, since backwards audio is generally recognisable as backwards.
