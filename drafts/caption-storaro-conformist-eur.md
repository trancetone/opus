Vittorio Storaro gave Ferdinando Scarfiotti the Moravia novel and the script for The Conformist (1970), and the production designer took him and Bernardo Bertolucci out to EUR, the quarter of travertine colonnades south of Rome that Mussolini commissioned before the war and left unfinished. Bertolucci wanted the film in real locations rather than on a stage, so the fascist architecture itself supplies the scale of the frames and the hard directional daylight falling through those arcades, conditions Storaro had to accept rather than build. Only one sequence went to Cinecitta, the radio station, and Storaro's account is that it went there because Italian law obliged the production to spend a few days in a studio rather than because the scene needed a stage. A quarter built to make a political idea visible goes on doing that work decades later for a film made against the regime that built it, which is why the location survey mattered more here than any lighting plan.
