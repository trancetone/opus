For the first twenty-five or thirty years of cinema a large proportion of film editors were women, and the reason given at the time was that the work resembled knitting or tapestry, since it consisted of taking lengths of fabric and joining them into a whole. The industry that employed them ran on the principle Jack Warner stated as not wanting it good but wanting it Tuesday, which required an industrial system, and cutting was the department where that system was absorbed. What changed the composition of the department was not a change in the work but the arrival of sound, because a soundtrack was electrical and therefore classified as technical rather than as sewing, and men began entering a job they had previously left alone. The work itself did not change when the department's composition did, only its classification, and a craft being reclassified as technical has often preceded a change in who is understood to be qualified for it.
