Haskell Wexler learned from Richard Burton's autobiography that Burton had argued against hiring him to photograph Who's Afraid of Virginia Woolf (1966), on the grounds that a cameraman out of gutsy newsreel work would show the pockmarks on his face and be unkind to him. Elizabeth Taylor took Wexler's side and was pleased with the result, but the trouble continued from the laboratory instead, because somebody there judged the New England footage too dark and there was talk of firing him, most of which he only found out afterwards. What he was actually doing was grading the darkness, holding several degrees of shadow and several degrees of fill across the night so that when the early morning light arrived the audience would register a change of time without being told one. A laboratory reading a deliberate scale of underexposure as a fault is the recurring condition of the job, and Wexler's own summary of the production is that there was a lot of heat on that film.
