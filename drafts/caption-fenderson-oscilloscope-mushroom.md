An oscilloscope in X-Y mode has no idea that it is displaying music, because it is doing the only thing it ever does, deflecting its beam horizontally according to the voltage on one input and vertically according to the voltage on the other. Jerobeam Fenderson feeds the left audio channel into the horizontal and the right channel into the vertical, at which point the drawing becomes arithmetic performed in sound, since a sine wave against a cosine of the same frequency traces a circle and the volume of the two channels sets that circle's size and shape. Adding a sawtooth to the right channel winds the circle out into a spiral, and multiplying the left channel by a sine at the sawtooth's own frequency, using only the final quarter of that sine, bends the spiral into the stem and cap of a mushroom that then moves. What makes this different from a music visualiser is that nothing is being interpreted, because no software is deciding that a loud passage should look like one thing and a quiet one like another. The two voltages that would drive a pair of speakers are the same two voltages driving the beam, so the image is not a picture of the sound but the sound arriving at a different instrument, which is also the geometry John Whitney was drawing with on the Vertigo titles.
