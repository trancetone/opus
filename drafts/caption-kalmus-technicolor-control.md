Natalie Kalmus, who held colour control over Technicolor productions as the company's consultant, is described here arriving on set in the brightest clothes imaginable and telling the art director that a wall was too blue or too green and would have to be altered, then redesigning the scheme around it. The resistance named in this account is specific, because it holds that she never came onto a Michael Powell set on the grounds that Powell would have thrown her out, and that Alfred Junge would never have agreed to his colours being changed by anyone. What the speaker draws from that is a claim about national practice, that the English use of Technicolor by cinematographers like Jack Cardiff had more to do with painting and with emotion than the American approach did. The American cinematographers are granted their brilliance in the same breath and then set aside as a different type of commodity.
