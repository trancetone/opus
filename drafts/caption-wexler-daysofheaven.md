Haskell Wexler shot the last nineteen days of Days of Heaven (1978) because Nestor Almendros was contracted to Francois Truffaut and had to leave before the picture was finished. Wexler went to the laboratory first and watched the footage Almendros had already exposed, then took the film over.

He handheld the Panaflex himself for the steel mill sequence that opens the picture. He also put diffusion on the lens, which Almendros had used nowhere in the film, and he is plainly uncomfortable about it on camera: he says he walks out of films that use a fog filter, that the effect is too easy, and that he felt he was violating a fellow cameraman.

Almendros won the Academy Award for the cinematography. Wexler's credit reads additional photography. The diffusion is in the finished film, and nothing in the credits marks where one cinematographer's work ends and the other's begins.
