Haskell Wexler took over photography on Days of Heaven (1978) for the final nineteen days of the shoot, Nestor Almendros having been contracted to Francois Truffaut and obliged to leave before the picture was finished, and his account of the handover begins at the laboratory, where he was shown the footage Almendros had already exposed.

Inheriting a film photographed across most of its length is a problem of continuity before it is a problem of style, and Wexler shot his own sequences, including the steel mill material that opens the picture, handheld on a Panaflex, departing from his predecessor in a single respect he describes as a source of guilt. Almendros had used no diffusion anywhere in the film and Wexler put it on the lens, despite stating that he leaves any picture using a fog filter within ten minutes because the effect is too easily obtained, and despite feeling, in his own phrase, that he was violating a fellow cameraman. The Academy Award for the cinematography went to Almendros while Wexler's credit reads additional photography, an arrangement that leaves the diffusion in the finished film with nothing in the titles to mark which passages carry it.
