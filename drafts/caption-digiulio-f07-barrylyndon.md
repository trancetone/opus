Ed DiGiulio, who ran Cinema Products Corporation, published his own account of the two lenses he built for Barry Lyndon (1975) in American Cinematographer, and the useful thing about that account is that it opens with him telling Stanley Kubrick the job could not be done. Kubrick had obtained Zeiss Planar 50mm lenses at f/0.7, originally made as still camera optics for NASA, and sent over the dimensional specifications, from which DiGiulio reported back that fitting one to a Mitchell BNC was impossible, because the barrel was too large in diameter and the rear element sat only about four millimetres from the film plane. What he did instead was rebuild the camera's receiving mount around that single lens and dedicate the body permanently to it, which inverts the usual arrangement in which lenses are made to suit the cameras that already exist. For wider coverage he took a second 50mm and fitted a reduction type projection lens over the front of it to produce an effective 36.5mm, and because the lens had to be wound fully clear of the mechanism before the camera could be racked over, he added a safety interlock in the form of a microswitch that would not trip until the lens had been rotated out nine complete revolutions. Sources disagree on how many of these lenses ever existed, variously ten made with six going to NASA and three to Kubrick, or three simply left over from a batch NASA had already taken.
