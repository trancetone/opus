Before shooting The Taking of Pelham One Two Three (1974), Owen Roizman took two Panavision cameras down into a subway station, set them side by side with the same lenses on both, and ran them at the same time on the same subject of passengers coming and going and boarding trains. Running the test as a simultaneous comparison rather than as two separate demonstrations is the whole point of it, because the only way an argument about anamorphic against spherical gets settled is if the thing being compared is the one variable and the light, the crowd and the moment are held identical. They screened the two reels the next day, and his account is that the room was unanimous and that the verdict was anamorphic. The second decision is less visible and did more work, because he knew from having shot in the stations before that there was light enough for his exposure, and what he lacked was any detail down in the shadows. So he flashed the negative, pre-exposing the stock to a controlled amount of light before it ever reached the camera, which lays a thin floor of density into the shadow area, so that a frame which would have given him bright highlights against dead black now opens up and lets a viewer see into the dark.
