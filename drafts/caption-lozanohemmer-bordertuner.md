Border Tuner put searchlights along the line between El Paso and Ciudad Juárez, and Rafael Lozano-Hemmer describes the aim in a way that inverts what a searchlight is for, which is that the beams were there not to look for individuals but to look for the relationships between individuals. A searchlight exists to find a person and hold them in it, so aiming one at the space between two people instead is a reassignment of the instrument rather than a new use for it, and the artwork becomes the connection the beams make rather than anything either beam lands on. He is explicit that the project became an artwork for listening, which sounds like a contradiction until you notice that the light is only the visible half of a channel carrying voices in both directions across a border. The strategy underneath it is stated as a deliberate bait, because he says the plan was to use the language of technology and spectacle and scale to bring people into the area, and then once they are standing there to give them something completely different. What he wants in place of spectacle is what he calls eccentric interaction, a moment where it is not business as usual, which is as good a working definition as any of why somebody puts an artwork in a public square instead of a room people have chosen to enter.
