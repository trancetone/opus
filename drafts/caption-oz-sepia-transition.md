The moment The Wizard of Oz (1939) turns from sepia into colour is a colour shot the whole way through, which is precisely the opposite of what it appears to be. Everything before it is genuinely sepia, printed that way, and the archivist Robert Harris confirms that early prints run sepia right up to the transition, so the trick is that the transition shot itself was photographed in Technicolor on a set painted sepia and the join becomes invisible. Judy Garland's double wears a sepia dress with sepia makeup, opens the farmhouse door onto Munchkinland and steps out of frame, the camera moves forward through the doorway after her, and Garland walks in behind it in the blue gingham. The studio had considered stencilling the sepia onto the film and abandoned the idea, because hand-stencilling in 1939 would have run to hundreds of hours against a staging solution costing a coat of paint and one costume. Sources differ on the double's name, variously given as Koshay and Korshay, and on whether she was a double or a stand-in, which is worth noting because she performs the most quoted colour transition in cinema and is not on the credits.
