Es Devlin designed the revolving cuboid that Beyoncé performed inside on the Formation Tour, and the detail she singles out about it is not the scale of the thing but a single slit cut into one face of it, an aperture that the light comes out through. The reason for that aperture turns out to be a story she tells about Beyoncé as a child, who spoke to a preacher on television, was told by him to put her hand on the screen, and felt the sensation of prayer when she did it. Devlin's reading of that moment is precise rather than sentimental, because what she describes happening in a child's mind is a combination of what a television physically does and what the language and tone of prayer feels like, which are two quite different kinds of reaching toward somebody. The object follows from the reading rather than decorating it, since a sealed block that revolves slowly and lets her image escape through one narrow opening is treating the performer as something being transmitted rather than something being looked at, and an aperture does that where a flat screen facing the crowd would not. She puts the conclusion plainly, that the big revolving block of her is another way of broadcasting her and what she is saying, which makes the design a claim about what a screen is for rather than a claim about how a stage ought to look.
