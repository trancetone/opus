The Joshua Light Show has been performing live behind musicians since the late nineteen sixties, and the decision at the centre of its method is a refusal, because the visuals are deliberately non-specific and deliberately not programmed, so that nothing in them is cued to a particular moment in the music. The company's own account of why is that the audience then all see their own light show, which is a different proposition from illustration, and it describes its position as being much more in the world of art than in the world of music, providing a background that works like a frame. The cost of that is the thing most people would reach for first, since a visual that is not programmed cannot hit a downbeat, cannot land an accent, and cannot be rehearsed against the arrangement at all. What makes the refusal legible is what it replaced, because the account of a concert before this work describes a band walking out to perhaps two follow spots and a black curtain, and then suddenly a visual as powerful and as strong as the music being played. The group says that as the music got better they got better, which locates the practice as a response rather than a fixed design, and it is worth knowing that this started on the Lower East Side before the Fillmore existed, built on what was already working in San Francisco.
