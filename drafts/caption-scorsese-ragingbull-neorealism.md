Martin Scorsese, describing the scenes in Raging Bull that happen outside the ring, says the approach came from wiping away all the technique he had thought about before and going back to an impression formed when he was five or six years old, watching Italian neorealist films on television. He names Paisà, Open City and Bicycle Thief as the pictures that set it, which locates the look of the domestic scenes in postwar Italian cinema rather than in any American tradition of boxing pictures. The argument that follows between Jake and his brother about the Janiro fight and the weight is the demonstration, and it was shot with only one camera on the actor at all times. The consequence was that the immediate response of the other actor was never recorded, so the exchange had to be assembled afterwards like a jigsaw puzzle, which took a long time to do.
