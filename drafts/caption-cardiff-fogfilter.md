Jack Cardiff, BSC shot Deborah Kerr on her last day of production on Black Narcissus (1947) through a number two fog filter, a diffusion element mounted in front of the lens that scatters highlights and lifts the darkest values, softening the image by a degree Cardiff himself calls almost unnoticeable.

Technicolor had forbidden it. The company sold the three-strip process on clarity and colour fidelity and kept its own consultants on set to protect that look, so filtration read as damage rather than as a choice. A message came through the next morning saying the previous day's work was completely ruined. Cardiff describes feeling sick, and the rushes were brought over specially to be screened.

Michael Powell watched them, said it was exactly what he wanted, and turned to Technicolor to tell them it was about time they learned something about art in movies. The dispute is an early instance of a cinematographer claiming authority over the image against the laboratory that processed it.
