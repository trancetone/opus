Michael Chapman operated the camera on Jaws (1975), and when Steven Spielberg said he did not want the frame wandering all over the place, Chapman answered that holding a steady horizon was not the way to make a sea picture and would have the audience throwing up in the aisles. Once the production moved onto the open ocean almost everything was handheld, since the Steadicam did not yet exist, and Spielberg later described the result as the most expensive handheld movie ever made, a joke that ran on set for the length of the shoot. The decision Chapman singles out is not the handheld work but the height, because he kept the lens at water level whenever conditions allowed, so the surface sits across the bottom of the frame for most of the picture. A viewer does not register that choice as a choice, and that is what makes it work, since a horizon held level with the water leaves the audience permanently aware that something could be directly beneath it.
