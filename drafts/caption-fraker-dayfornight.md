The Professionals (1966) was the second picture William A. Fraker operated for Conrad Hall, with Jordan Cronenweth as assistant cameraman and Charles Rosher Jr on the second camera, a crew in which three men would go on to photograph features of their own. Fraker's difficulty with the film was the sheer amount of night in it, night being what he calls a permanent conundrum in photography, since there is no version of it that a camera records the way an eye accepts it. The escape from the compound, where the men take a coal cart and go out into the dark, was not shot at night at all but in the last of the daylight and then printed down to read as night. What Fraker credits Hall with is not daring on its own but the judgement to hold a balance between black and white, colour, day and night, and to be innovative while remaining, in his word, solid.
