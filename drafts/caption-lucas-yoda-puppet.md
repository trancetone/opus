George Lucas, describing where he stood when he started work on The Empire Strikes Back, identifies the problem as one he had created for himself, because the training and exposition the second film needed had been designed for Ben Kenobi and he had killed Ben off in the first picture. What that left him holding was a quantity of instruction scenes with no character available to perform them, so the requirement became a new Jedi master who had to be more powerful than the one he had removed and interesting to watch besides. His apprehension was about the form rather than the writing, since the real question was whether a principal character in a feature film could be carried by a small rubber puppet. By his own account it looked like it was going to be a disaster until the moment the footage came back with the puppet on film and talking, Frank Oz working him, after which it read on screen as magic.
