# The Caption Pattern, as Published

Derived by reading the captions actually posted, not from either written spec.
Where this disagrees with `caption-voice-spec.json` or the `miac-content`
skill, this file describes what the account does; the specs describe what it
was told to do. See `spec-conflicts.md`.

## Shape

**Roughly 175 to 200 words. Four movements. No opening aphorism.**

### 1. Opening: named people, named work, what was done technically

Never an abstraction, never a general maxim, never a rule stated in the
abstract. The first sentence names the maker, usually including the technical
collaborator, gives the work and its year, and states what was built or done.

- "John Boorman and cinematographer Alex Thomson built *Excalibur* (1981)
  around a consistent green light treatment applied across the film's
  exteriors..."
- "Gordon Willis, ASC shot *The Parallax View* (1974) in anamorphic
  Panavision..."
- "On *McCabe & Mrs. Miller* (1971), Robert Altman and cinematographer Vilmos
  Zsigmond developed the technique of flashing..."

The performance analysis says to state a rule rather than a fact. That rule
lives in the **structure of the whole caption**, not in an opening maxim. A
caption opening "A shadow on the floor can disclose the machine that made the
shot" is a hook line, which the voice spec bans outright.

### 2. The apparatus

How it was physically achieved. Equipment, measurements, materials, process.
Specific enough to be checkable.

Green gel reflected at the prop whenever the sword is unsheathed. Pre-exposing
the negative to a controlled amount of light. A single overhead diffusing rig
of the cinematographer's own design.

### 3. The complication

A counter-account, an institutional reaction, a scholarly dispute, or a
production constraint. This is what separates these captions from technique
summaries, and it is present in nearly every strong post.

Warner Bros. executives reading the dailies as ruined footage. Jacques
Deslandes arguing Méliès studied an Alfred Clark print rather than discovering
the stop trick by accident. Mark Minett's statistical comparison against
seventy contemporaneous films. Five months of Irish rain the production leaned
into.

Named scholars and named executives. Not "some critics."

### 4. The close

Flat and analytical. States what the technique established or what it does
structurally. Never a punchy landing line, never a rhetorical flourish.

- "...establishing the basic grammar, halt the camera, change the scene,
  resume, that special effects filmmaking continued to build on for decades."
- "...to hold the story outside any specific historical period and inside the
  register of legend instead."

## Hashtags

Six to eight. Mostly proper nouns: person, film, named technique. Close on
`#FilmHistory` and `#MovingImageArts`. Never the generic set in the voice
spec's never_include list.

## Register notes

- No em dashes anywhere
- No second person, no rhetorical questions, no hook lines
- Long, complex sentences are correct when the idea warrants them
- Every factual claim verifiable: names, dates, measurements, equipment
- The modern parallel, if present at all, is the final sentence. Most posts
  omit it entirely
- Never "MIAC"
