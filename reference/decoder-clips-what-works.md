# Decoder Clips — What We Learned

What actually drives performance on @moving.image.arts.
Based on 55 published reels, 1 Aug – 9 Sept 2026. Account went 696 → 3,466 followers in 16 days, with 33 unfollows total.

---

## The core finding

**A clip performs when its first sentence hands the viewer a mechanism they can use on something else.**

We called these decoder clips. The viewer leaves slightly better at watching everything, not just better informed about one film. That is the whole distinction, and it is worth more than subject, length, timing, or fame combined.

| | Median views | Follows | Saves | Held past 3s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Decoder** (19 posts) | **2,954** | 145 | 454 | 60.3% |
| **Survey** (18 posts) | 261 | 12 | 35 | 44.5% |

**11× the reach. 12× the followers. 13× the saves.**

---

## The three parts of a decoder

**1. State a rule, not a fact.**
A rule has an *if this, then that* inside it that the viewer can run themselves.

- Works: "Colour tells you whether you're inside her head or outside it." → Kieślowski, 2,894 views
- Fails: "Nam June Paik is widely credited as the originator of video art." → 114 views

**2. Prove it inside the clip.**
The viewer should verify it with their own eyes or ears, not take your word. The best post shows one unchanged face reading three different ways depending on what's cut against it. Nothing to trust, you just see it happen. It holds the account's second-highest save count.

**3. Leave the rule portable.**
The example is a delivery vehicle. The rule is the product. If it only explains that one film, it's trivia. If it changes how someone watches the next thing they open, it's a tool, and tools get saved.

**The test, before cutting anything:** *Could someone use this tonight, on a film I never mentioned?*

---

## The single biggest lever: sentence one

The mechanism has to be the **first sentence**, not the payoff.

| Caption opens on | Median views | Held past 3s | Avg watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| The mechanism | **6,057** | 59.3% | 20.8s |
| A person or a survey | 968 | 43.3% | 11.6s |

**6.3× from word order alone.**

The clearest proof is the Greenaway post. Best-researched caption on the account — colour assigned by room, costumes changing as characters cross the restaurant, Vermeer's key light measured at four and a half feet. It opened "Peter Greenaway trained as a painter before turning to film." Average watch: **10.8 seconds**. Nobody reached the good part. Jarman, posted the same day with the same construction, held 9.2 seconds.

Both posts *contained* a decoder. It was sitting in sentence two.

## The accidental controlled experiment

Same cinematographer, same account, nine days apart.

| | Caption opens on | Views | Held past 3s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gordon Willis · *All the President's Men* (17 Aug) | "structures its visual language around a contrast between two lighting environments" | **528** | 24.7% |
| Gordon Willis · *The Parallax View* (26 Aug) | negative space registering exposure before the plot confirms it | **41,799** | 60.5% |

**79×.** Everything except the caption structure was held constant.

## What kills a clip

- **Opening on a biography.** "X trained as…", "X is widely regarded as…", "X describes…"
- **Surveying.** Two careers in one post (Coates & Fields, 260 views). Four films in one post (Kubrick, 564). One mechanism, one work.
- **Taxonomy instead of operation.** Listing categories rather than giving one thing to do. Eisenstein's five montage types: 1,351, the weakest of its batch.
- **Vague nostalgia.** "Wondering where these now-40-somethings are" → 23.9% hold, the worst on the account. Specific nostalgia works; wistful nostalgia dies.
- **Generic hashtags.** #Storytelling #Innovation #CreativeProcess #ContentCreation #FutureVibes. These cluster on the worst performers. Proper nouns instead: #GordonWillis #CitizenKane #DeepFocus.

---

## What we ruled out

Every intuitive explanation was tested and failed.

- **Length** — r = +0.06. A 28-second post and a 172-second post both broke out.
- **Time of day** — medians within noise.
- **Caption opener structure as grammar** — a mechanical test on "opens on person vs work" predicted nothing. The two biggest posts both open on a person's name. The distinction is semantic, not syntactic.
- **Fame of subject** — Kubrick (564) and Monty Python (383) died. A 1928 Dada short did 3,271.
- **Hashtag specificity alone** — real but weak, r = +0.33. Not the driver.

## Subject: real, but secondary

Subject is roughly a 1.7× multiplier. Caption form is roughly 7.8×.

| | Decoder | Survey |
|---|---|---|
| Canon narrative film | **2,262** | 290 |
| Avant-garde / video art | 1,322 | 203 |
| Internet / contemporary media | — | 249 |

**Eight canon-film posts still died** — Kubrick, Griffith, Gilliam, Godard, Schoonmaker, Willis-as-survey. Subject can't rescue a survey.

**Light, colour and sound outperform editing and camera.** But the requirement is *demonstrable in the clip*, not *visible in a frame* — a post about Altman's eight-track sound recording did 50,641 views, and sound isn't visible at all. Editing sits at a 1,413 median because relationships between shots take longer to see than a green filter does.

**Internet-media subjects are tested and lose.** Five posts, none above 266, including one written as a correct decoder. The reason is structural as much as editorial: #FilmHistory is a niche a small account can surface in; #ContentCreation is not.

## The thesis is a landing, not a launch

The Moving Image Arts argument — historical visual language shaping contemporary tools — only works as the **final sentence**.

| Where the modern parallel sits | Result |
|---|---|
| Closing line (Brakhage, Varda) | 4,417 / 1,800 |
| Opening line or core claim (Soviet montage, Buzz pastiche) | 263 / 254 |

Don't post about TikTok. Post about Kuleshov and let the reader draw the line. The account's best post never mentions the internet at all — it explains how juxtaposition manufactures meaning, which *is* the argument, delivered as film history.

Roughly 90% film craft, one sentence of landing.

## Voice

From the house style, and consistent across all 55 published captions.

- No em dashes. Ever.
- No strings of short punchy fragments building to a landing line. That reads as ad copy.
- No second person, no rhetorical questions, no hook lines.
- Open with a substantive statement of fact.
- Sentences can be long and complex when the idea warrants it. Don't force staccato.
- Analytical and precise. Clever-sounding is not the goal.
- Body under 80 words. Hashtags separate, 5–7, mostly proper nouns.

## Things worth knowing about how it travels

- **3-second hold rate is the leading indicator.** Known within hours, predicts final reach better than anything else. Below 45%, a post will not travel.
- **Posts accrue for 3–5 days.** Excalibur went 30,645 → 70,672. Don't judge anything at 24 hours.
- **Cadence drives totals more than quality does.** Three to four reels a day produced 251,451 views in five days. Three reels over six days produced 7,819. The formula held in both windows; only volume changed.
- **Paid amplifies conversion, it doesn't create it.** All three boosted posts sat *below* the organic engagement median. Rank boost candidates by save rate and hold rate, never by views.
- **Audience is 51% US / 49% international.** Any geo-specific ask reaches about 5–7% of your viewers.
- **Stories reach ~1% of followers.** 747 views across 23 stories at 3,466 followers. An audience acquired from one viral reel will see you in feed and never open Stories.
- **Event posts convert at 20.9 follows per 1,000 views vs 4.5 for craft posts** — and show roughly 10× fewer views. Judge them on the ask, not the reach.

---

## The one honest caveat

The decoder/survey labels were assigned by hand after seeing the numbers, so some circularity is unavoidable. Three things make it more than a story: every mechanical test failed, watch-time data that hadn't been seen when the grouping was made split the two groups identically (60.8% vs 45.0%), and nine posts written deliberately to the rule afterwards produced a 3,521 median with a floor above every survey post ever published.

n = 55, one account, six weeks. The effect sizes are large enough to act on and the sample is too small to generalize past this account.
