# Source Registry

Series and documentaries where practitioners narrate over their own footage.
This is the preferred source shape: primary-source analysis, footage that
demonstrates it, and speech for Opus to curate and caption from, all
pre-assembled by people who held the rights to cut them together.

## The screening test for a series

**Does footage run under the voice, or is it a person in a chair?**

A talking head cannot demonstrate anything. This single test decides whether a
series is a sourcing source or only a research source.

## Verified: footage runs under the narration

| Source | Covers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Visions of Light** (1992) | Cinematography, silent era through late 80s | The model. Already in use, so ration it. |
| **The Cutting Edge: The Magic of Movie Editing** (2004) | Editing, 1903 to 2003 | Dir. Wendy Apple, narrated by Kathy Bates. The editing equivalent of Visions of Light, and editing is currently underserved. On Netflix, Starz, MUBI. |
| **Side by Side** (2012) | Film versus digital capture | Dir. Christopher Kenneally. Many DPs. Subject is a transition rather than a technique, so screen segment by segment. |

## Unverified leads, worth checking

Named from general knowledge, not confirmed in research. Check the footage test
before use.

- AFI Harold Lloyd Master Seminars, decades of archival craft talks
- Directors Guild Visual History oral histories
- Criterion Collection supplements and interviews
- BAFTA masterclasses
- Academy Originals short craft pieces
- Kodak and manufacturer craft series (Panavision, Arri, Cooke)
- Studio making-of featurettes attached to specific restorations

## Why diversify off a single source

Three reasons, in increasing order of seriousness:

1. The footage starts to look the same, since one documentary has one grade,
   one interview setup, and one era of transfer.
2. The audience recognises the well. A feed visibly drawn from one film reads
   as a clip account rather than an editorial one.
3. It is a single point of failure. One rights holder, one detection profile,
   one takedown affecting everything.

Target no more than roughly one post in four from any single source.

## Gap

No verified source yet where **Freddie Young** narrates over the *Lawrence of
Arabia* mirage shot. He died in 1998 and his surviving accounts are text oral
histories, which do not help a reel. Subjects whose practitioners died before
the video-interview era will keep hitting this, and that is a reason to check
source availability early rather than after the research is done.
